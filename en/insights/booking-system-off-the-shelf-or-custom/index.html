Booking systems for hostels, swim schools and courses: off-the-shelf or custom?

A hostel books beds and rooms, a swim school fills groups and lanes, a course organiser keeps track of places and teachers. They all need to take bookings, but the needs differ more than you might think. Here is how you can think when you choose.

Published 2026-10-20 by Masood Ahmad

Off-the-shelf services often go a long way

There are many ready-made booking services, and for many businesses they work well. You pay a monthly fee, set up times and prices, and can take bookings online more or less straight away.

If your bookings follow a common pattern, for example one room per night or one place in a group, a ready-made service is often the wisest first step.

Before you decide, it is a good idea to test the service with a few real bookings. Check how cancellations work, whether customers can book easily on their phones, and whether you can get your own data out, for example as an Excel file, if you want to switch later.

Signs that an off-the-shelf service is not enough

It is time to consider something of your own when you notice that much of the work still happens outside the booking service:

  • You have rules the service cannot handle, for example levels, age groups or prerequisites.
  • One booking needs several resources at the same time, such as a room, staff and equipment.
  • You keep lists in Excel alongside the booking service.
  • Bookings and payments have to be moved by hand into the accounting system.
  • Customers still call or email, because the booking flow does not suit them.

Reminders, resources and payments

Reminders by email or text message reduce no-shows and save phone calls. What matters is that the reminder goes out automatically and contains the right information, for example time, place and what the customer should bring.

Resource planning is about making sure the same room, lane or teacher is not booked twice. The more resources that depend on each other, the more important it is that the system understands how they connect.

Payment at booking reduces last-minute cancellations. If the payments also produce records for the bookkeeping, you avoid reconciling by hand afterwards.

A middle ground: build around what you have

It does not have to be either-or. Often you can keep a ready-made booking service and add to it, for example with a link to the accounting system or a simple overview of occupancy.

A fully custom booking flow is most worth it when booking is the core of the business and the rules are so specific that no ready-made service fits.

Whichever route you choose, think about who will look after the system afterwards. A booking flow that stops working on a Saturday morning costs both customers and trust.
